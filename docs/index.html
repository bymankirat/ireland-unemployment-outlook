Irish Unemployment, September 2026: Trends and a 3-Month Outlook

Mankirat Singh · Portfolio · GitHub · LinkedIn

This is an independent analysis using open data from the Central Statistics Office (table MUM01). It is not an official CSO publication.

Key findings

  • The seasonally adjusted unemployment rate was 5.0% in September 2026, unchanged on the month and up 0.1 pp on the year, with about 150,000 people unemployed.
  • This compares with a peak of 16.1% in December 2011 and a low of 4.1% in July 2022.
  • Young people (15-24) had a rate of 12.5%, 3.1 times the rate for those aged 25-74 (4.0%).
  • The rate for men (4.9%) was 0.3 pp below the rate for women (5.2%).
  • Outlook: the unemployment rate is forecast at 5.0% in December 2026 (80% interval 4.5-5.4%).

2. The long view

Since January 1998 the rate has ranged from 3.9% (October 2000) to 16.1% (December 2011). The average over the last 12 months was 4.9%, against 4.6% in the 12 months before.

3. Young people

Youth unemployment is consistently higher. Today's ratio of 3.1 compares with an average of 2.5 since 1998.

4. Men and women

The gap between men and women was widest in December 2009 (6.5 pp higher for men), during the downturn that hit male-dominated sectors such as construction hardest. It has since closed: men's and women's rates have been almost the same, on average, over the last five years. Today women's rate is 0.3 pp higher.

6. How good are these forecasts?

Four simple, transparent models were compared. Every month from 2012, each model was fitted only on the data available at that time and asked to forecast the next 3 months. The model was chosen on 2012-2019 (96 forecast dates, excluding the COVID-19 period) and then judged once on later years it never saw.

On 56 monthly forecasts made from January 2022 onwards, the chosen model did not consistently beat simply assuming no change: its average error was 9% larger 1 month ahead, 20% larger 2 months ahead and 12% larger 3 months ahead. That is a real finding: month to month, the seasonally adjusted rate moves very little, so "no change" is a hard benchmark to beat.

Background notes

  • Source: CSO table MUM01, Seasonally Adjusted Monthly Unemployment, downloaded through the CSO's open data API (PxStat). Rates are for ages 15-74 unless stated.
  • Seasonal adjustment is carried out by the CSO; no further adjustment was applied.
  • Revisions: the CSO revises recent months as new Labour Force Survey results arrive. The backtest uses today's revised figures, not the figures available at the time, so real-time accuracy would be somewhat lower.
  • COVID-19: from March 2020 the CSO also published COVID-adjusted measures. This analysis uses the standard series and excludes the pandemic months from model selection.
  • Methods: Python (pandas, NumPy), matplotlib. Models: no-change, 12-month drift, AR(2) on monthly changes, and damped-trend exponential smoothing with parameters chosen by least squares. Code: GitHub.
